Why it's built this way
Private

Your audio and transcripts never leave your computer. There's nowhere for them to go, so nothing can be logged, reviewed, or used to train anyone's models.

Offline

After a one-time model download it needs no connection. It keeps working on a plane, in a booth with dead wifi, or in a building that blocks the outside.

Bought once

Buy it once and it's yours. No subscription, no usage limits, nothing metered by the minute, the word, or the file.

What it does
Paired entries, live

It listens to the speaker and, at each natural pause, writes that whole phrase as one clean block with its translation beside it (source and meaning always lined up, never a stream that rewrites itself).

Recorded files too

Drop in a recording (wav, mp3, m4a, flac, ogg) and it transcribes and translates the same way, then saves a plain-text transcript right next to the original.

Export and autosave

Keep a plain-text transcript of any session. Long jobs autosave to disk as they finish, so a slow render never loses your work.

Hears the room, not you

Point it at any input device, so it transcribes the speaker you're interpreting for, not your own voice coming back over your headset.

How it works

One phrase at a time, on purpose.

01

Listen

It buffers the audio quietly while someone is speaking.

02

Wait for the pause

When they stop, it knows the phrase is finished, not mid-pause.

03

Transcription / translation

It transcribes and translates that phrase once, and shows the pair.

The delay is about half a second to 5 seconds, depending on your machine, per phrase. In exchange you get stable, aligned text instead of a live stream that jitters and rewrites itself.

Privacy

After downloading, runs offline.

The first time you run it, it downloads the models it needs (around 1.5 GB), so it does need a connection to get set up. Once that's done, it works entirely on your machine.

No account, no server, no telemetry. Your audio and your transcripts stay with you, because there's nowhere else for them to go.

What to expect

A safety net, not a replacement for you.

Rexicon runs a mid-sized model tuned to keep up with a live speaker on an ordinary laptop, and that tuning costs accuracy. Expect mistakes on fast speech, strong accents, several people at once, background noise, numbers, names, and unusual terminology, often the moments that matter most.

The translation is machine translation: fine for the gist, but it won't catch register, idiom, or a deliberate ambiguity the way you will. Treat it as a second pair of ears and a memory aid, and don't put it in front of a patient or a courtroom and step away.
